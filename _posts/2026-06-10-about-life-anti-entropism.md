# About life anti-entropism

I wondered - _what happens to fish when they come out of water?_
The practical answer: fish breathe through gills, from which they extract oxygen. 
Out of water, the gills collapse: they can no longer function and the fish can no longer breathe. 
Then we know that out of water the skin dehydrates, altering the balance of salts and fluids in the cells. 
It must always stay moist. Water also provides buoyancy; without it, the organs would collapse under their own weight.

I am very sorry for this imaginary fish, it didn't deserve to meet this end for the sake of our reflection, 
but from here I began to think that **Nature is magnificent**: there are beings that live in totally different environments. 
We have fish, birds, mammals, some living in the sea, some in fresh water, some living at night, some without sunlight.
In short, life always finds a way to exist.

So I was thinking about Darwin, and about two things that leave me astonished
-  What is the origin of the first spark of life? _Which is a bit like the spark of the universe itself_
- The idea of precision, sometimes even mathematical but certainly engineering,
- of living beings and their systems, which seem almost designed by a great engineer.

I have no particular faith, indeed what distinguishes me most is a certain agnosticism, but let's reason a little.
Thinking that it is only chance that generates all of this is equivalent to betting that, 
if you leave lifeless chemical matter $k$ in a space $s$ for a time $t$ greater than or equal to 13.4 billion years, 
upon your return, you will certainly find something animated.
Assuming this concept, a *principle of convergence toward life* must necessarily exist: 
an ordering principle must exist - the Greeks called it λόγος - if, from the simple, we arrive at the complex. 
Otherwise Darwinian theory would make no sense. And this force, or tendency, must lead matter to aggregate as an anti-entropic principle 
- unlike the second law of thermodynamics.

When I studied biology at school I was deeply struck by the perfect structure of DNA and cells. 
Thinking that this is only chance is absurd. Assuming that a cell exists, 
the idea that masses of them aggregate and form something more complex is by no means obvious, quite the contrary. 
But it shows how matter tends to *complexify*. If it were governed only by chance, 
life should not tend toward survival and perhaps we would have one species, perhaps even a weak one, and perhaps not even self-aware.
